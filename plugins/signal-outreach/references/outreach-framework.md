# Outreach Framework

Use this framework to write outreach sequences that are respectful, personalized, and grounded in public context.

## Core principles

1. **Evidence-grounded:** Every message references a specific public signal. No coldGeneric outreach.
2. **Respectful of time:** Short messages, one question per touch, easy to ignore.
3. **Never automated without consent:** This skill writes drafts. The human sends them.
4. **No private information:** Only reference what the prospect posted publicly and intentionally.
5. **Tone-matching:** Mirror the prospect's communication style from their public content.

Everything from "Channel strategy" through "Response handling" below applies to **Individuals** only. Segments get a content/GTM brief (see "Segment content briefs") and Companies get a BD pitch (see "Company BD pitches") — neither gets a multi-touch message sequence.

## Channel strategy

### Email

When to use: The prospect has a visible work email, or their company page has a contact path.

Structure:
- Subject: 4-8 words, references their specific situation (not "Quick question")
- Opening: One sentence acknowledging their public context
- Body: One sentence connecting their pain to the product
- CTA: One low-friction question

Rules:
- Never use "Hi {first_name}" personalization tokens — write the actual name.
- Never use tracking pixels or read receipts.
- Subject line must be honest — no clickbait.
- If you can't find a real email, do not guess. Use an alternative channel.

### LinkedIn

When to use: The prospect posts on LinkedIn, or their profile is public and active.

Structure:
- Connection note (if not connected): 2-3 words referencing shared context
- First message: Same structure as email but more casual
- If they engage: Follow up with a longer value-add

Rules:
- Never send InMail to someone who posts actively (use their comments instead).
- If you're not connected, engage with their content first (1-2 genuine comments) before messaging.
- Connection notes: max 300 characters. Reference a specific post or shared interest.
- Do not automate connection requests at volume.

### Twitter/X

When to use: The prospect posts publicly about the problem or uses Twitter for professional discussion.

Structure:
- Public reply: Add value to their tweet (a resource, a perspective, a relevant data point)
- DM (only if they're active and open): Reference the public tweet, ask one question
- Thread: Write a relevant thread and @ mention them naturally (only if the thread has standalone value)

Rules:
- Never DM someone who doesn't follow you unless their DMs are explicitly open.
- Public replies must add value even if the prospect never responds.
- Do not reply to every tweet — pick the most relevant one.
- No "Great tweet!" engagement farming.

### Community reply

When to use: The prospect posted in a forum, Reddit, Indie Hackers, Hacker News, or similar.

Structure:
- Reply in-thread: Answer their question or acknowledge their pain, then mention the product if it's genuinely relevant
- If the community allows it: Share a brief case study or data point
- If they engage: Continue the conversation naturally

Rules:
- Respect community self-promotion rules. Many forums require you to disclose affiliation.
- Do not create accounts specifically to post about your product.
- If the thread is old (30+ days), do not revive it. Find a fresh touchpoint.
- The reply must be valuable to other readers, not just the prospect.

### GitHub

When to use: The prospect has an open issue, feature request, or discussion related to the problem.

Structure:
- Comment on the issue: Acknowledge the problem, share how the product addresses it (if relevant)
- If they maintain a repo: Open a discussion or issue if there's genuine overlap
- If they're a contributor: Engage with their PR or code first

Rules:
- Never open issues just to promote a product. The overlap must be genuine.
- Comments must add technical value, not marketing copy.
- If the issue is a bug report, do not use it as a sales opportunity. Help with the bug first.

## Message construction

### First touch formula

```
[Public context reference — 1 sentence]
[Pain connection — 1 sentence]
[Product in one sentence — no jargon]
[Low-friction question — 1 sentence]
```

Total: under 90 words.

### Follow-up 1 (Day 3)

```
[Brief acknowledgment — 1 short sentence]
[New angle: insight, data point, or relevant example — 2 sentences]
[Revisit the question or ask a new one — 1 sentence]
```

Total: under 70 words.

### Follow-up 2 (Day 7)

```
[No ask — pure value]
[Share something useful: article, template, benchmark, relevant thread — 2 sentences]
[Light touch: "Thought you might find this useful" — 1 sentence]
```

Total: under 60 words.

### Breakup (Day 14)

```
[Acknowledge they're busy — 1 sentence]
[Leave the door open — 1 sentence]
[No guilt, no pressure]
```

Total: under 40 words.

## A/B variant strategy

### Variant A: Direct

Lead with the pain signal. Connect immediately to the product. Best for high-intent and trigger-present prospects who are actively looking.

### Variant B: Curiosity

Ask a question about their situation. Reveal the product only if they engage. Best for problem-aware and potential-fit prospects who may not know a solution exists.

Selection matrix:

| Prospect stage | Recommended variant | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| High intent | A | They're looking. Be the answer. |
| Problem aware | B | Pull them in with a question. |
| Trigger present | A | Timing is now. Don't waste it. |
| Potential fit | B | They may not know they need this. |

## Response handling

### Positive reply

- Thank them for the response.
- Suggest a 15-minute call to learn more about their workflow.
- Provide a scheduling template: "Here are a few times that work for me: [2-3 options]. What works best?"
- If they suggest a different time: be flexible.

### Question reply

Common questions and responses:

| Question | Response approach |
|---|---|
| "What is this?" | One-paragraph explanation. Link to the product page. No pitch. |
| "How much?" | Share pricing transparently. If there's a free tier or trial, mention it. |
| "Is this relevant to X?" | If yes, explain specifically how. If no, say so honestly. |
| "Who are you?" | Brief, honest introduction. No title inflation. |
| "Do you have case studies?" | Share 1-2 relevant examples. If none exist yet, say so and offer to show the product. |

### Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Already have a solution" | Acknowledge it. Ask what's working and what's not. Offer a comparison only if there's a real gap. |
| "Not the right time" | Respect it. Ask when would be better. Set a calendar reminder to follow up. |
| "Too expensive" | Understand the budget. Offer alternatives (free tier, manual workaround, phased adoption). |
| "Need to talk to my team" | Offer to prepare a one-pager or brief they can share. Make it easy for them to champion. |

### No reply after sequence

- Do not re-engage for 90 days.
- Alternative touchpoints during the wait:
  - Comment genuinely on their public content.
  - Share relevant content publicly that they might see.
  - Attend their event or talk and engage naturally.
- After 90 days: one final touch. If no reply, move on permanently.

## Segment content briefs

A Segment is an audience or demand pattern, not a person — there's no one to message. Distilled from content-strategy practice, reframed for a single evidence-backed pattern rather than a full content calendar.

### Searchable vs. shareable

Every brief opens by naming which kind of angle the segment supports (a segment can be both):

- **Searchable** — captures demand that already exists. Someone is actively typing a query. Write for the exact query pattern signal-scout identified (e.g., "[competitor] alternative," "[pain] tracker app").
- **Shareable** — creates demand that doesn't yet exist as a search. A counterintuitive insight or an original data point worth spreading beyond the people already looking.

### Buyer-journey mapping

Match each content piece to a stage, using the query language that stage actually uses:

| Stage | Query modifiers | Content type |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | "what is," "how to," "why does" | Explainer post, definitional page |
| Consideration | "best," "vs," "alternatives," "comparison" | Comparison page, roundup |
| Decision | "pricing," "reviews," "worth it," "discount" | Pricing page, case study, review response |

### Building the brief

For each Segment, produce:

1. **Angle type** (searchable / shareable / both), carried over from signal-scout's classification.
2. **2-3 concrete content pieces**, each tagged with its buyer-journey stage and format — not "write blog posts," but "a [Product] vs. [Competitor] comparison page targeting consideration-stage searchers."
3. **Target keywords** in the segment's own language (not product jargon), pulling from signal-scout's `target_keywords` plus any long-tail variants found during research.
4. **Channel plan** — where this audience actually spends time: SEO/blog, ASO copy, a named subreddit or Discord, YouTube comparison videos, a specific newsletter or podcast.
5. **Proof points** — evidence-backed claims from signal-scout's `proof_points`, ready to drop into copy. Never invent a statistic to fill this in.

No opener, no follow-up cadence, no A/B variant — a Segment brief is a plan someone executes over weeks, not a conversation.

## Company BD pitches

A Company is evaluated as an org-level account or partner, not an individual to court through a drip sequence. Distilled from co-marketing/partnership practice, reframed around public-signal sourcing only — no paid firmographic or contact-enrichment tools, and no personal or scraped executive contact information under any circumstance.

### Combined value proposition

State plainly what the partnership unlocks that neither side has alone — not "synergies," but the specific gap it closes (e.g., "our pantry-recognition layer plus their existing coaching loop closes the one input that's still manual today").

### Naming the execution path honestly

Carry over signal-scout's classification and don't inflate it:

- **Self-serve program** — an open developer platform or partner-apply page exists. Lowest friction: apply directly.
- **Warm BD** — a known partnerships/BD function exists (a partnerships page, a named team). Route through their public contact form or published contact.
- **Cold BD** — no visible partner motion. State this plainly as a longer shot, not a warm lead, and size the ask accordingly (a smaller first ask, not a full partnership proposal).

### Writing the pitch

One paragraph, under 90 words:

1. One sentence naming the combined value proposition.
2. One sentence naming the specific evidence (the trigger or gap) that makes this timely.
3. One specific, single ask — a self-serve signup, a named program application, or a specific intro request. Never "let's explore synergies" or "would love to chat."

### Contact path — public only, no exceptions

Use only a public contact path: developer platform docs, a partnerships page, a published BD contact form or email. Never suggest:

- A named executive's personal or scraped email address.
- Cold-emailing an assistant, gatekeeper, or generic info@ inbox as a workaround.
- Any data-broker or contact-enrichment tool to find a "better" contact.

If no public contact path exists, say so in the pitch record and mark the execution path as cold BD rather than inventing a contact.

### Fallback ask

Every pitch includes a smaller secondary ask for if the primary one goes nowhere (e.g., a newsletter mention or a co-authored post instead of a full integration). Try the fallback once after ~30 days of silence, then stop — no repeated cold outreach into an org that hasn't engaged.

## Quality checklist

Before delivering outreach output:

**Individuals**
- [ ] Every first touch references a specific public signal by name or topic.
- [ ] No message exceeds its word limit.
- [ ] Tone matches the prospect's public communication style.
- [ ] A/B variants are meaningfully different in approach, not just wording.
- [ ] Follow-ups add new value, not repetition.
- [ ] The breakup is genuinely graceful.
- [ ] No private information is referenced.
- [ ] No message claims to have been sent.
- [ ] Response handling covers positive, question, objection, and no-reply scenarios.
- [ ] Channel selection matches the source type.

**Segments**
- [ ] Angle type (searchable/shareable) is named, not assumed.
- [ ] Every content piece names a buyer-journey stage and a concrete format.
- [ ] Keywords are in the audience's language, not product jargon.
- [ ] Proof points are evidence-backed, never invented.

**Companies**
- [ ] The pitch has exactly one specific ask, under 90 words.
- [ ] Execution path is stated honestly (self-serve / warm BD / cold BD).
- [ ] Contact path is public only — no personal, scraped, or enrichment-tool-sourced contact.
- [ ] A fallback ask exists for a non-response.
